nimisin, and Ones I Actually Like

What is a nimisin?

If you're reading this article, it's really unlikely you need a full write up on what toki pona, the constructed language started by Sonja Lang in 2001, and nimisin are, but in case you do, here's a quick summary.

toki pona as a conlang is special due to it having a comically small "word inventory", on the order of ~130 total words. For context, Classical Latin, a very old language with a very low word count, has ~40,000. English today has ~880,000.

"Digi, if there's so few words in the language, how do you speak in it, and also why do you not know the number exactly?"

I'll answer the second question first: basically, there's a lot of contention as to which words "count." The original book "Toki Pona: The Language of Good" (also called pu) lists 123 words. The later "Toki Pona Dictionary" (also called ku) lists 137. The site nimi.li lists 119 "core" words, plus 10 "common" words, plus 12 "uncommon" words, for a total of 141. Then, there's 37 "obscure" words, all with at least over 5% usage. Then, there's the whopping 188 "sandbox" words; of course a lot of those are jokes, and none of them see much use.

As for how to speak the language? I don't have the time, frankly, to explain that one. TL;DR: you have to use the small amount of words to explain what you mean. Sure, there's no word for cake, but I can say "pan suwi musi" (lit. "fun sweet bread") and you'll get what I mean. If we were already talking about birthdays, you'll definitely get what I mean.

So what are nimisin? nimisin (from the words nimi (meaning "word") and sin (meaning "new") refer to "new words" in the language, but the word has a... derogatory feel to it. On the whole, the majority of toki pona speakers don't love the idea of nimisin, or if they do, they use a very small amount of them. Why? Because toki pona has a philosophy of minimalism.

Now, frankly, I'm not going to get too much into the philosophy stuff. You can have an interesting discussion about minimalism, if toki pona is a good example of it, if it even should be minimalist, and if it does a good job at either minimalism or what it set out to do. I'm not really interested in that discussion; frankly, toki pona has grown well past its creator, and I think it's more intersting to discuss what it is vs. what it was/is "trying" to be. It's a language people use to speak, and its design choices affect the way it's spoken in good and bad ways.
